About Me

I am a Research Scholar at the National Institute of Science Education and Research (NISER), Bhubaneswar, working in Numerical Cosmology.

My research focuses on understanding the formation and evolution of dark matter halos and their connection to galaxy formation and evolution, the large-scale structure, of the Universe. I use cosmological N-body simulations to investigate halo morphology, assembly history, mergers, environment and clustering.

I am particularly interested in going beyond the conventional spherical description of dark matter halos, studying their triaxial structure and how morphology evolves across cosmic time. I also investigate how mergers and the surrounding environment influence halo properties and morphology-dependent clustering.

Other areas of my research include the halo-galaxy connection, splashback, large-scale structure and the use of numerical simulations (N-body and Hydrodynamical) to connect theoretical models with observable properties of galaxies and halos.

Research

01

Structure of Dark Matter Halos

Studying the distribution of halo shapes and its evolution in scale-free Einstein-de Sitter models and ΛCDM. Shape parameters follow universal distributions across epochs, and oblate, triaxial and prolate halos each follow a distinct universal distribution, with abundances that are universal in peak height.

02

Dark Matter Halos Beyond Ellipsoidal Structure

Decomposing the 3D density field of halos into spherical harmonics to measure how far they deviate from a perfect ellipsoid. The deviation is larger for unrelaxed halos, grows when satellites are included, and the anisotropic terms vary systematically across halo morphologies.

03

The Role of Mergers and Environments in Shaping Halo Morphology, and the Correlation between Assembly Bias and Halo Morphology

Following halos of 1011–1015 M⊙/h along their main progenitor branches to see how mergers and the local tidal environment shape morphology and spin. Secondary halos mostly enter along the host’s major axis, and oblate halos, though rarest, cluster most strongly. Concentration parameter has shown a morphology dependent trend which has connection with assembly bias.

04

Splashback Surface of Dark Matter Halos

Investigating the splashback boundary of dark matter halos and its connection to halo morphology.

05

Gas Structure in Dark Matter Halos

Using the NINJA simulation suite to study how AGN feedback reshapes the joint gas–dark matter distribution in halos. Halos classified by central black hole activity show that feedback-dominated halos have stronger gas–dark matter dissociation and suppressed star formation.

06

Box Size Affecting Halo Shapes

Measuring how simulation box size changes halo axis ratios q and s at fixed mass, using two suites of MP-Gadget simulations. Median q and s differ by a few percent at low mass and by about 5–10% at the high-mass end, likely from missing long-wavelength tidal modes.

Halo shapes

Dark matter halos are triaxial, with axes a ≥ b ≥ c. The triaxiality parameter T = (1 − q²) / (1 − s²), with q = b/a and s = c/a, sorts them into oblate, triaxial and prolate shapes.

Illustrative particle models, rotating about the short axis c. Drag a halo to turn it.
